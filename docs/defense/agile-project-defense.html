Speaker notes

45 seconds. Open with the decision: a team has dozens of practices it could improve, but limited capacity. Which two practices should it consider next? This project turns the organization's recorded improvement history into a ranked recommendation. I built a functional system and evaluated its historical alignment with later improvements. The evaluation supports feasibility and motivates a prospective pilot. It does not establish that receiving a recommendation causes faster adoption. Defense 05
Speaker notes

105 seconds. Similarity uses cosine similarity of maturity profiles, keeps distinct peer teams and weights their observed improvement magnitudes. The transition signal counts transitions between consecutive improvement-bearing steps. It skips steps without improvement and imposes no ordering on practices that improve in the same step. Popularity captures organization-wide trends and provides a strong simple comparator. Every signal uses historical evidence bounded by the baseline. The blend scores are ranking values. A score of 0.75 does not mean a 75 percent chance of success. The exact normalization rules differ by component and are available in the report and backup.

Defense 06
Speaker notes

90 seconds. The grid covers peer count, minimum similarity, the three component weights and popularity recency. Selection optimizes the mean monthly Conditional Hit Rate@2 over completed earlier prediction months. All teams use one global policy for that month, which reduces the amount of available data needed compared with per-team fitting. The target month's outcomes never participate in selecting its policy. With little history, the fallback is popularity. Defense 09
Speaker notes

120 seconds. Be precise about two clocks: the prediction month and the team's most recent earlier baseline. Recommendation scoring uses the earlier baseline state, not the target month's maturity values. Comparable snapshots and sequence learning precede the baseline. Peer follow-up ends at the baseline. Policy selection uses only completed earlier prediction-month outcomes. After producing the recommendation, evaluation looks at up to the first three recorded team snapshots after the baseline, beginning with the prediction snapshot. This is why it is inaccurate to call the evaluation window simply the next three calendar months after prediction. These controls guard against future information entering scoring or selection. Outcome conditioning still limits interpretation, which the next slide makes explicit.

Speaker notes

120 seconds. Define a hit as a nonempty intersection between the two recommended practices and eligible practices that later improve. The measure is conditional on observing at least one improvement. It does not give an unconditional success probability for any team. The primary reported cohort contains 121 outcome-bearing cases over five globally complete months. One case has fewer than three subsequent team snapshots. In the stricter audit requiring three subsequent team snapshots, there are 298 cases: 120 with an improvement and 178 without one. Do not silently add the 121 and 178 because they refer to different completeness definitions. Without records of attempted practices, the no-improvement cases cannot establish intervention success or failure. The conditional cohort evaluates identification of observed successes.

Backup 18
Speaker notes

The worked score uses rounded normalized component values. Scrum Master has components 0,1,1 and score 0.750. Tech debt strategy has components 0.513,0.525,0.933 and score about 0.726. Cosine compares profile direction and can underrepresent absolute maturity differences, which is a limitation to acknowledge. Dividing all maturity vectors by 3 does not change cosine similarity. The transition model aggregates empirical paths, not causal dependencies. A step can contain multiple improved practices. Count the cross-product between consecutive improvement-bearing steps, without ordering practices inside one step. Component-specific normalization occurs before blending. The implementation does not normalize the final blend again.

Backup 21
Speaker notes

Recommendations are fast after caches warm, but first-time policy search evaluates up to 675 policies per prediction month. Do not claim linear scaling or production readiness. The service loads data in memory, uses cached scoring and offloads heavy backtesting to a single-worker executor. The prototype lacks hardened authorization, per-team isolation and operational monitoring. A wider rollout would need deployment, backup/recovery and refresh procedures. These are appropriate engineering next steps and do not change what the current implementation demonstrates.
